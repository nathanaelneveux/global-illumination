# Research and implementation decisions

This page distinguishes implemented techniques from possible future work.
The [architecture](architecture.md) is the current design; the
[historical research notes](history/research-design-notes-2026-10.md) preserve
source reviews, experiments and rejected alternatives at their recorded dates.

## Implemented

- **Nested axis-aligned distance fields.** Region, brick and voxel bounds
  accelerate traversal. Uniform regions avoid dense payloads, unchanged
  occupancy reuses local bounds, and scene deltas drive sparse GPU uploads.
  Geometry is prepared on CPU workers, not generated by a GPU world engine.
- **Surface radiance cache.** Supported resident face estimates terminate
  secondary rays, so multiple bounces propagate over successive updates.
  This is a biased iterative cache; the analytic furnace and native cache
  tests qualify its energy behavior. A finite three-hit oracle is not its
  final-lighting reference.
- **View-owned lighting.** World-space face identities and histories are
  retained per camera; a two-way world cache preserves nearby offscreen
  estimates. Small projected faces use per-pixel histories instead. This
  bounds lighting state by viewport and cache radius, while geometry still
  follows host page residency.
- **Sampling.** Emitter sampling uses scrambled Sobol pairs; scattering and
  roulette retain their qualified Halton dimensions. Emitter next-event
  estimation and cosine paths use balance MIS. Cross-face path resampling,
  learned guiding and ReSTIR reservoirs are not production features.
- **Retention.** Geometry and sunlight changes carry spatial bounds and flux
  estimates. GPU passes cap effective history using measured variance and
  predicted change; unknown flux uses the distance curve. Source/model
  changes have separate revision rules. [Retention research](retention-research.md)
  records the derivation and qualification.
- **Presentation.** Connected coplanar reconstruction uses measured variance
  and bias; temporal display filtering is separate from raw measurements.
  Direct sunlight uses shared voxel corners. Filtering does not improve the
  raw estimator's independent sample count.

## Comparison sources

[NAADF](https://github.com/cg-tuwien/NAADF) motivates nested traversal and
screen-scaled distant lighting. Its renderer retains and reconnects individual
paths, including proposal and visibility accounting. This implementation
shares those broad goals but does not implement NAADF's spatial resampling.
The [source comparison](naadf-comparison.md) is a versioned study, not a claim
that the two renderers have equivalent costs or storage.

[vxray and its development log](https://nelari.us/post/voxel-ray-tracing/)
provide useful comparisons for voxel traversal, stable sampling and radiance
caches. [DDGI](https://jcgt.org/published/0010/02/01/paper-lowres.pdf) and
[SVGF](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-07_spatiotemporal-variance-guided-filtering-real-time-reconstruction-path-traced)
illustrate explicit visibility, history and reconstruction policies. Their
probe/image representations are not the algorithms implemented here.

Solari's versioned source is another recursive-cache comparison. Adopting a
technique from a hardware-ray-query renderer requires independent validation
of its sampling, storage and platform assumptions. The historical notes record
which versions were read; they are not a survey of every current release.

For reuse experiments, preserve path geometry, proposal densities, source
support, visibility and zero-valued samples. Compare raw matched prefixes
separately from reconstructed gameplay captures. The failed fixed-bin guide
and fresh-path candidates are recorded in [experiment history](history/experiments.md).
They do not establish that all path guiding or resampling approaches fail.

## Indirect corners

A direct sun ray asks about a known direction, so its visibility can be shared
at a corner. Indirect light is a hemisphere integral whose normal, connectivity
and origin matter. Position-only corner values cannot represent hard edges.

The topology census in `reconstruction.rs` compares the current face-oriented
representation with oriented connected corners. It is a state-count test,
not a speed benchmark. A corner-transport experiment would need its own
storage, visibility and raw-estimator qualification. The default remains
face transport with corner reconstruction.

## After 0.1

0.1 ships on Bevy 0.19 with all three crates (`bevy_voxel_world` 0.17 still
requires Bevy 0.19). 0.2 targets Bevy 0.20 (released 2026-10-08). When porting,
confirm that [bevy#26246](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/26246) is
included: 0.20's GPU-allocated mesh uniforms can draw meshes with another
mesh's material for a frame or two at startup.

Experiments after 0.1, each judged against the same scenes on the M4:

- **Scene builds that cost the change, not the world (decided 2026-11-02).**
  Every scene build rebuilds the top-level empty-space field and copies the
  region map for the whole loaded world, and probably repeats more
  whole-world work. With `VOXEL_GI_LAB_FAR=64` (about 98,000 pages), builds
  take over a second at 283k regions and 3 to 5 s at 566k. That bounds far
  streaming and edit latency for every host with a large world (`3b4b5f6`
  commit message; `world::stream_bench`). Profile a far-world build first,
  then make the region map persistent and update the top-level field
  locally. Judge by edit response in a far world (`response`, `tunnel`
  profiles with `VOXEL_GI_LAB_FAR`) and by how quickly far lighting fills in.
  It also shortens the black flashes along LoD band edges: a remeshed
  chunk's faces are not GI's until the next build lands. A shader fallback
  for them cost 1.5 ms or more per frame in every form tried, and was
  reverted (`7e8c034`).

- **Selective GI geometry coverage (proposed 2026-11-03; untested).**
  The BVW connector declares every loaded chunk, and GI retains that geometry
  beyond the 64-voxel world-cache radius. Visible receiver identities and
  far-tier surface positions already come from raster depth and normals;
  the broader geometry serves lighting rays. Hypothesis: offscreen geometry
  farther behind the camera than the cache radius often contributes little
  to mid-tier indirect light, and the far tier may tolerate still less detail
  or a smaller transport neighborhood. The cache radius has not been qualified
  as a geometry cutoff.

  Measure page ingestion, scene-build, upload and memory costs alongside where
  lighting rays travel. Experiment with GI geometry admission independent of
  host residency, retaining visible surfaces and varying offscreen coverage
  or detail by tier. Consider bounded detailed bounce geometry with cheaper
  long-range occlusion and important-source coverage. Distant sunlight blockers,
  bright emitters and the combined influence of large surfaces need explicit
  checks; omitted geometry must not silently become known empty space.
  Compare against full host coverage with the preferred corner look fixed:
  settling and reveal/return in house/cave, response/tunnel edits, motion and
  frame-time tails, including far-world builds and distant blockers/emitters.
  Record any visible bias and boundary popping alongside savings. This is a
  future experiment, with no geometry policy or default changed.

- **MetalFX denoising.** [metalfx_wgpu](https://github.com/mate-h/metalfx_wgpu)
  wraps MetalFX's temporal denoised scaler (macOS 26, wgpu 30, so Bevy 0.20).
  Since July, Solari runs on Metal
  ([bevy#25123](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/25123)), and MetalFX
  support for it is an open PR
  ([bevy#26306](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/26306), tested on an
  M5, no published quality or performance figures). Two questions: whether
  MetalFX can denoise and upscale the noisiest signal (the far tier's
  per-pixel light, whose motion flicker is deferred), and how Solari with
  MetalFX compares with voxel GI on the same scene and hardware. MetalFX needs
  motion vectors, which the BVW material does not produce today.
- **Separate static and dynamic emissive history.** Long, cheap accumulation
  for static emitters (ore veins, panels) and fast response for moving lights
  (a suit lamp). The idea comes from
  [IgorKonovalov/voxel-engine](https://github.com/IgorKonovalov/voxel-engine),
  a wgpu ray-marched voxel engine with screen-space reservoir reuse and probes.
  Its v0.36 work (plan 0047) did three things:
  - moved emissive accumulation from `rgba16float` to `rgba32float`, because
    half floats stop accumulating once history grows: the gap from an f64
    replay went from 13.92% to 0.0001% (lavapipe);
  - added an emissive-only gradient guard that re-shades last frame's point to
    raise the blend where light changed;
  - spread probe updates by a hash, so neighbouring probes no longer update
    together and pop in patches.

  Its next spike (plan 0051, approved but not started) adds a world-space
  emissive cache keyed by surface voxel that excludes the moving headlamp:
  this resembles the world cache here, with an additional static/dynamic split.
  Our accumulations are already f32 (`ViewEstimate`, `PixelHistory`, counts
  to 65,536); only the far tier's display output is half precision, and it is
  never accumulated. Our edits invalidate history explicitly instead of
  estimating gradients. To do: measure how quickly a moving `VoxelGiEmitter`
  responds, then dig a one-voxel and a 10x10 opening and compare convergence,
  stale-light removal, memory and GPU time.
- **Stretch: atmosphere transmittance for GI's suns.** GI imports each sun's
  colour from its `DirectionalLight`. Under Bevy's atmosphere, raster lighting
  reddens at sunset while GI's sun would not: the mismatch Solari fixed in
  [bevy#26127](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/26127). Apply the same
  transmittance when importing suns, and check a sunset sweep against raster
  lighting.
